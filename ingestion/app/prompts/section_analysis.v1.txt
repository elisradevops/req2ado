IMPORTANT on the type hierarchy: Epic groups Features (and MAY group other Epics, see below), Feature groups Requirements. A Requirement's parent must ALWAYS be a Feature or another Requirement (a child Requirement, see below) -- never an Epic directly, and never null unless this Requirement is genuinely the section's only node. An Epic's children must ALWAYS be Features or other Epics -- never a Requirement directly. If a section's content only warrants one level below its root (e.g. a root Feature with only atomic requirements under it, no separate Epic), that is fine -- the point is never to skip a level in the chain that IS present: once you emit an Epic, every Requirement under it must go through an intermediate Feature, even a single one grouping all of that Epic's Requirements.

The hierarchy is not always exactly 3 levels deep -- use these three additional structural tools whenever the section's content genuinely calls for them, not by default:
- Nested Epic: when a section spans multiple large, clearly distinct functional domains, emit an intermediate Epic per domain under the section's own root Epic, rather than putting every Feature directly under one flat root (e.g. a 'Television Receiver' root Epic split into 'Broadcast Reception', 'System Settings', 'System Information' child Epics, each then grouping its own Features). An Epic grouping other Epics is NOT capped at one level -- if one of those child Epics is ITSELF large enough to represent multiple distinct sub-initiatives (by the same scope test above, applied again one level down), split it into its own further child Epics before reaching Features. How many levels of Epic-under-Epic are warranted depends entirely on the section's actual scope, not a fixed depth -- most sections need zero or one level of Epic nesting, but do not artificially flatten a genuinely multi-tier domain just to stop at one level.
- Nested Feature: when a sub-topic under a Feature is itself a coherent capability with several Requirements of its own, emit it as a Feature nested under that Feature, not flattened into a sibling Requirement list (e.g. 'Picture Modes' as a Feature nested under a 'Display' Feature, itself grouping 'Movie Mode'/'Game Mode' Requirements, instead of six flat Requirements directly under 'Display').
- Child Requirement: when a Requirement's exceptions, conditions, alternative flows, or specific follow-up behavior are themselves separately testable, emit them as Requirements parented under that general Requirement, not as siblings under the same Feature (e.g. a general 'Automatic Switching to an Active Source' Requirement with child Requirements 'Multiple Active Sources' and 'User Dismisses the Suggestion' for its two specific sub-cases).
Do not force any of these three when the section's content is genuinely flat -- a Feature with only atomic, unrelated Requirements and no real sub-capability or exception case should stay exactly that flat, not be padded with an invented nested level.
